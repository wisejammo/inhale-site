Privacy Policy

Inhale — breathwork and breath-hold training for iPhone and Apple Watch
Last updated 6 October 2026

Inhale collects nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. I run no servers, and nothing the app records is ever sent to me. Your data stays on your own devices, in Apple Health, and in your own iCloud account — all under your control, none of it visible to me.

What the app stores, and where

Inhale saves the following on your iPhone and, if you use it, your Apple Watch:

  • Your breathing sessions — technique, duration, cycles completed, and any breath-hold times
  • Your BOLT test scores
  • Presets and routines you create
  • Your settings, such as whether sounds and haptics are on

This data lives in the app's own storage on your devices. It is covered by your device's encryption, and it is included in an iCloud or local device backup if you have backups switched on. I never receive it and have no way to see it.

iCloud sync between iPhone and Apple Watch

So your iPhone and Apple Watch agree, Inhale keeps a small copy of your BOLT scores, presets and routines, plus how many free Apple Watch sessions you have used, in Apple's iCloud key-value storage. This is stored in your iCloud account, by Apple, and is visible only to Inhale on your own devices — I cannot see or access it. Your session history is not synced this way. If you are not signed in to iCloud, nothing syncs and each device keeps its own copy.

Apple Health

If you grant permission, Inhale uses Apple Health in these ways:

  • Writing: a breathing session longer than one minute is saved as Mindful Minutes.
  • Apple Watch sessions: a session of a minute or more started on your Apple Watch is also saved as a Mind & Body workout, with the heart rate your Watch measured during it. This lets it count towards your Activity rings and appear in the Fitness app.
  • Reading: heart rate samples recorded around a session, so the app can show your heart rate before and after; Mindful Minutes, to show your history; and Inhale's own Mind & Body workouts, so sessions done on your Watch appear in History on your iPhone.

Health data is handled entirely by Apple's HealthKit on your device. It is never transmitted anywhere, never shared with any third party, and never used for advertising or marketing. You can grant or revoke each permission at any time in Health → Sharing → Apps → Inhale, and Inhale continues to work with Health switched off entirely — you simply lose the Mindful Minutes logging and the heart rate readout.

Purchases

Inhale is free on iPhone. If you choose to leave a tip, or to unlock the Apple Watch app after its free trial, the purchase is made through the App Store and handled entirely by Apple. I never see your name, your Apple ID or your payment details — Apple reports sales to me only as totals. A tip unlocks nothing and changes nothing in the app; it is simply a thank-you.

What is not collected

  • No name, email address, or account — there is nothing to sign up for
  • No analytics, crash reporting, or usage telemetry
  • No advertising identifiers, and no advertising
  • No location data
  • No contacts, photos, microphone, or camera access
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind

Deleting your data

Deleting the app from your iPhone and Apple Watch removes everything it stored on them. The small synced copy in your iCloud account can be removed in Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage. Anything already written to Apple Health belongs to Health rather than to Inhale, and is managed there — open the Health app, find Mindful Minutes or Workouts, and delete whichever entries you want.

Children

Inhale is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Breathing exercises and your safety

Inhale is a training tool, not a medical device, and nothing in it is medical advice. Never practise breath holds or power breathing in or near water, while driving, or while standing. Stop if you feel dizzy or unwell, and speak to a doctor before starting any breathing practice if you are pregnant or have a heart, lung, or blood-pressure condition.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new date at the top. Since the app collects nothing, any change is likely to be a clarification rather than a change in what happens to your data.
